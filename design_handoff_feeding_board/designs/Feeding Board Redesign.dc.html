2TV display — the night-drive cluster

Read from the doorway, not from a chair

Recreated from display/display.html: ten pens in two rows, per-dog portion, the +P / +S flags, the five scale tiers that shrink cards as pens fill, the footer counts, and the NOT LIVE staleness banner with the old board still showing behind it.

The one idea

A car dashboard at night shows you almost nothing — until something needs you. So here a clean bowl is dim and a problem glows. A dog that ate everything is quiet warm grey on near-black. A quarter eaten warms up. A refusal sits on a deep terracotta card with a slow amber pulse, and its pen lights a warning lamp beside the pen name so you can find it from across the room without reading a single word.

That inverts the old screen, where every card was equally bright and the refusals had to compete with nineteen dogs who were fine.

Details worth knowing

  • Everything is sized in cqh — 1% of the display’s own height — so the same page fills the 32″ 1366×768 set, a 1080p panel and a 4K panel identically. No more pixel sizes tuned to one television.
  • Your five scale tiers are kept, keyed on the busiest pen, just re-expressed in cqh. Quiet day: big names. Packed day: everything shrinks together and still fits.
  • “Need a look” in the footer counts refusals and quarters — the only number on the screen anyone has to act on. The progress bar is gone, since you feed in sequence and don’t need it.
  • Warm dark, not grey dark. The ground is the Organic system’s deepest neutral (#201e1d), the accents are its terracotta and sage lifted to their light steps so they glow on black. No new colours.

1Feeding board — tablet-first redesign

What I read, and what I assumed

Everything here comes from your index.html, CLAUDE.md and HANDOVER.md: ten fixed pens (top-1…5, bottom-1…5), five feeding statuses, meal type, prescription + four supplement types, staging area, delete bin, within-pen ordering via position, the offline banner, and Submit gated on online AND empty queue. Nothing about the data model or the sync contract changes.

The Apple-ish part

You asked for Apple’s philosophy and the project is bound to the Organic system, so I read “Apple” as behaviour rather than chrome: one obvious thing per screen, progressive disclosure, 44px minimum targets, motion that explains what moved, and no decoration that isn’t doing a job. The paint is Organic — cream ground, terracotta, sage, Caprasimo over Figtree, big radii. Emoji are gone; the icons are Lucide-style at stroke 2.75.

Three decisions worth arguing with

  • Portion is one tap, always visible. The <select> became a five-segment control on every tile — All · ¾ · ½ · ¼ · None. It is the job staff repeat 20 times a round, so it costs one tap and never opens a menu. Status is doubled in colour and position, so it survives sunlight and colour-blindness.
  • Medicine and supplements are folded away. Tapping a tile expands it; a set flag shows as a small badge next to the name. That is what buys the density — a bottom pen holds five dogs in ~430px instead of ~670px, so both pen rows fit a 10″ landscape screen with nothing off-screen.
  • Long-press is the only way to pick a dog up. Your 300ms hold, anywhere on the tile — no grip handle, no second gesture to learn. The tile visibly tightens the moment the press lands, so you know it took before you move. See 1b for how it survives Android without one.

What’s live in these frames

All four device frames are the same component, independently interactive: hold a tile to pick it up, drop between tiles to reorder, drop on the bin or the staging area, set portions, expand a tile, add a dog by typing, keyboard-move with Space + arrows, and open Preview / Add today’s dogs. Board contents are dummy data.

The drag, and the Android bug

Your comments call the current handlers “deliberately hand-balanced”. The failure they describe — a drag that dies mid-gesture on Android — has three causes in the old code, and this version removes all three.

  1. The non-passive touchmove is registered at mount, not mid-gesture. Chrome decides whether a gesture is a scroll on its first moves; a listener attached 300ms late can no longer cancel one. Registered up front, every move after the press lands is cancelled at source.
  2. setPointerCapture on the handle. Moves keep arriving at the same element even when the tile re-renders, the list reflows, or the finger leaves the pen — the old code lost the drag when React replaced the node.
  3. No body{overflow:hidden}. That lock is what makes the page jump under the finger on Android. Instead the board auto-scrolls when you drag near an edge, so an off-screen pen is reachable in one gesture.

Plus: tiles keep touch-action:pan-y so the board still scrolls when a finger lands on one — the press, not a dedicated handle, is what converts a touch into a drag; pointercancel lands the dog at its last good position instead of dropping the move; the ghost is transform-only inside one requestAnimationFrame; hit-testing is elementFromPoint so it stays honest under zoom; and Space + arrow keys do the whole move without a pointer.
